Mobile quick guide

Everything in your pocket.

Swipe through the essentials. Each card is a short, practical walkthrough you can finish in under a minute.

1

Install the app

Download PassGram for Android from the Apps tab, open it and sign in with your account. Allow notifications so login codes can reach you instantly.

2

Turn on biometrics

Enable fingerprint or face unlock in Settings. Codes and sensitive entries stay hidden until you confirm it is really you.

3

Scan to sign in

On any computer, open web.zpas.org and scan the QR code with the in-app scanner. Check the device and city shown, then approve.

4

Review and end sessions

Open Settings, then Devices to see every signed-in browser. End anything you do not recognise with one tap.

What if I lose my phone?

Sign in from another device you trust, open Devices and end the lost phone's session. Then change your password. Because new logins need approval on a device you already own, a stolen password alone is not enough.

Does it work offline?

Your vault entries on the phone can be opened without a connection. Signing in to the web, receiving codes and syncing need an internet connection.

Will notifications show my code?

No. Lock-screen notifications stay generic. The six-digit code appears only inside the app after you confirm with biometrics or your PIN.

How much battery or data does it use?

Very little. PassGram only wakes up when a login request arrives or when you open it, and messages are tiny.

Why PassGram

One calm place for everything you
sign in to.

Most of us juggle dozens of accounts, hundreds of passwords and a pile of verification codes spread across browsers, notes apps and screenshots. PassGram brings those pieces together so that signing in becomes quick, predictable and safe, whether you are on your phone, at your desk or on a borrowed computer.

PassGram starts from a simple idea: security tools should be pleasant enough that you actually use them. Instead of reusing the same password because remembering a new one is tedious, you let the vault create a long, random password for every service and fill it in for you. Instead of hunting for an authenticator app, your one-time codes sit next to the account they belong to.

Your account works across Android, Windows and the web. Sign in once on the official web app at web.zpas.org and you get the same familiar, chat-style interface you see on your phone. Everything is designed around clear screens, large touch targets and as few steps as possible between you and the thing you need.

We are also deliberate about what we do not do. Sensitive actions, such as approving a new browser or revealing a one-time code, are confirmed on the device you already own and trust, so a stolen password alone is not enough to take over an account.

How it works

Sign in to the web in
seconds, not minutes.

PassGram Web gives you two ways to sign in. Both are confirmed by your phone, so you never have to type a long password on a shared or unfamiliar computer.

Open web.zpas.org

Visit the official web app. You will see a QR code, and below it the option to continue with your phone number or email address. Always check that the address in your browser reads web.zpas.org before you continue.

Scan the QR code with the app

Open PassGram on your phone, choose the scanner and point it at the code on your screen. The app shows which browser and approximate location is asking to sign in, so you can decide whether it is really you.

Or receive a code inside the app

If you prefer to type your number or email, PassGram sends a six-digit code straight to the app on your phone instead of an SMS. The code is only revealed after you confirm with your fingerprint, face or device PIN, and it expires after one minute.

Review new logins in your security inbox

Every time a new device signs in, a notice is added to the security activity area of the app with the device name, location and time. If anything looks wrong you can end that session straight from Settings, then Devices.

Security you can read

Careful by default,
clear about the details.

We would rather explain how sign-in protection works than hide behind buzzwords. These are the principles the web app and the mobile app are built around.

1

Codes that live briefly

QR sign-in codes expire after ten minutes and work only once. As soon as a code is used, it is consumed on the server and cannot be replayed, even if someone photographed your screen afterwards.

App sign-in codes are stored only as a one-way hash and are limited to a handful of attempts before they are locked.

2

Approval on your own device

A new browser is never trusted on its own. The request is shown on your phone, and the code itself is revealed only after biometric or PIN confirmation, so someone holding your unlocked phone for a moment still has to prove who they are.

3

Sessions you can end

Each signed-in browser appears in your device list. Ending a session signs that browser out quickly, and a device that has been ended cannot quietly sign itself back in.

4

Location without tracking

When you allow it, your browser shares an approximate position so the login notice can show a city and country. We round it before sending and do not hand it to third-party map services. If you decline, we simply show that the location is unavailable.

5

Private by design

Secrets are protected before they are stored, and sign-in challenges are kept in server-only data that browsers cannot read or modify directly. Access rules are written to deny everything that is not explicitly needed.

6

Honest notifications

Notifications on your lock screen stay generic. The sensitive six-digit code is never printed in the notification text, so it cannot be read over your shoulder or captured by a screenshot of your notification shade.

Everyday life

Built for the moments
you actually have.

At home and at work

Keep personal and work logins separate while still reaching both from one account. Search finds an entry in a couple of keystrokes, and a favorites list keeps the accounts you open daily within a single tap.

On a computer that is not yours

Need to check something from a friend's laptop or an internet cafe? Open the web app, approve the sign-in from your phone, and end the session when you are finished. Nothing needs to be installed, and nothing needs to be remembered.

When you change phones

Because your account is not tied to a single screen, moving to a new device is a matter of signing in again and approving it. Your old session can be ended from the device list so that nothing is left behind.

REZ Encryption

If it is yours, it stays unreadable to everyone else.

REZ Encryption is the name of the protection that surrounds everything you keep and send in PassGram. It combines AES-256 encryption, device-held keys and direct device-to-device exchange, so your private content is locked before it ever leaves your phone. No outsider, no server, and no member of our own team can open it.

AES-256-GCM, end to end

Messages, notes and vault items are sealed with AES-256 in GCM mode. This is the same class of encryption that banks and governments trust, and it also detects any attempt to tamper with your data.

Device-to-device (P2P) keys

Chat keys are agreed directly between the devices of the people in the conversation. Only the public part is shared. The private part stays inside your phone’s secure hardware and never travels to us.

Layered key protection

Your master password is hardened by several stages, including Argon2id and PBKDF2 with 600,000 rounds, before it becomes a key. Guessing it by brute force becomes extremely slow and expensive.

Nobody can read it

Because we never hold your keys, we have nothing to hand over. A leaked database, a curious employee or a stolen server copy would show only scrambled data with no way to open it.

Private by default

You do not have to switch anything on. Protection is part of how PassGram stores and sends your content, on your phone, on the web and on desktop.

Approval on your device

New sign-ins are approved on a phone you already trust, with one-time codes delivered into your app instead of SMS, so a stolen password alone is not enough.

The trade-off of real privacy: because only you hold the keys, we cannot recover your vault if you lose both your master password and your recovery key. Keep your recovery key somewhere safe. Read the full security guide
